Solar Energy

Harnessing Kenya's Abundant Sunshine

Solar energy technology encompasses two primary pathways for converting sunlight into usable power. Photovoltaic (PV) systems use semiconductor cells to directly transform solar irradiance into electricity - a silent, scalable technology that works from a single rooftop panel to sprawling utility-scale arrays covering hundreds of acres. Our team at Ecotrunk has designed and supervised PV installations ranging from 10 kW commercial rooftops in Nairobi to multi-megawatt ground-mounted farms in the Rift Valley.

Kenya possesses some of the finest solar resources on the planet. The Great Rift Valley region receives insolation levels of 5.5 to 6.5 kWh per square metre per day - among the highest in the world. At current module prices below USD 0.15 per watt, unsubsidised solar electricity in Kenya frequently undercuts diesel generation by 60 to 80 percent, with simple payback periods of three to five years for commercial and industrial users.

The integration of battery energy storage systems is transforming what solar can deliver. By pairing PV arrays with battery storage, our projects can now deliver firm, dispatchable power that operates through the evening peak, stabilises voltage fluctuations, and provides backup during grid outages. Ecotrunk is actively deploying hybrid solar-plus-storage systems for commercial clients who demand 24-hour energy availability.

  • Commercial & Industrial Rooftop PV - Reducing operational energy costs for businesses across Kenya's urban centres
  • Utility-Scale Solar Farms - Ground-mounted PV installations from 1 MW to 50 MW with PPA financing
  • Mini-Grid & Off-Grid Systems - Decentralised solar for rural communities beyond grid reach
  • Solar-Plus-Storage Hybrids - Lithium-ion battery integration for firm capacity and grid support
  • Solar Water Pumping - Powering irrigation and agricultural applications across arid regions

Wind Energy

Capturing the Power of Kenya's Wind Corridors

Wind energy technology converts the kinetic energy of moving air into electricity using turbine generators mounted on tall towers. Modern onshore turbines typically range from 1.5 MW to 6 MW in rated capacity, with hub heights reaching 140 metres and rotor diameters exceeding 150 metres. The technological trajectory has been relentless - taller towers access stronger, more consistent wind speeds, while advanced blade aerodynamics and direct-drive generators have pushed capacity factors from 25 percent to over 45 percent in prime locations.

Kenya is blessed with some of Africa's most promising wind resources. The Turkana Corridor experiences powerful channeled winds averaging 11 to 13 metres per second during peak season - among the strongest measured on the continent. The Lake Turkana Wind Power project, with 365 turbines producing 310 MW, stands as Africa's largest wind farm. Our wind resource assessment work involves deploying meteorological masts and SODAR units to collect at least twelve months of site-specific data before committing to turbine selection and layout design.

Wind power integration into Kenya's grid presents both challenges and opportunities. The national grid was designed around predictable hydropower and geothermal baseload, so wind's variability requires sophisticated forecasting and dispatch scheduling. Beyond the technical, wind energy delivers profound community benefits. In Turkana, the wind farm has brought all-weather roads, mobile coverage, and employment to one of Kenya's most marginalised regions - demonstrating that community engagement is the foundation of long-term project success.

  • Onshore Wind Farm Development - End-to-end delivery from resource assessment to grid connection
  • Repowering & Retrofit Solutions - Upgrading existing farms with modern, higher-capacity turbines
  • Hybrid Wind-Solar Systems - Combining complementary profiles to smooth overall generation
  • Community Wind Projects - Smaller-scale turbines for rural electrification and productive uses
  • Wind Measurement & Feasibility Studies - Meteorological monitoring and financial modelling to de-risk investment

Hydropower

Leveraging Kenya's Rivers for Clean Baseload Power

Hydropower captures the energy of water moving from higher to lower elevations, converting gravitational potential energy into electricity through turbines coupled to generators. Run-of-river projects divert a portion of a river's flow without significant impoundment, offering lower environmental impact. Large reservoir projects provide multi-season water storage for dispatchable power on demand. Each configuration carries distinct trade-offs among capacity, cost, and environmental footprint. Our hydropower practice spans the full range, from pre-feasibility studies to detailed design and construction supervision.

Kenya's hydropower potential is concentrated along the Tana River basin, the Athi River system, and rivers draining the Aberdare Range and Mount Kenya. The Seven Forks cascade on the Tana River - comprising Masinga, Kamburu, Gitaru, Kindaruma, and Kiambere - has been the backbone of Kenya's electricity system since the 1970s, with a combined capacity exceeding 500 MW. Our work includes hydrological assessments for new run-of-river schemes in Nandi and Murang'a counties, and technical audits of existing plants to identify performance improvements. The strategic value of hydropower lies in its dispatchability - providing crucial grid stabilisation as Kenya adds more variable renewable generation.

Environmental and climate considerations are central to responsible hydropower development. Run-of-river and small-scale projects carry a lighter environmental footprint, making them attractive for rural electrification in Kenya's highland regions. Ecotrunk advocates for climate-resilient design: sediment management strategies, spillways designed for extreme flood events, and environmental flow releases to support downstream ecosystems. For remote communities, small hydro plants of 10 kW to 500 kW provide reliable 24-hour electricity - powering schools, clinics, and small enterprises long after commissioning.

  • Run-of-River Hydro Schemes - Environmentally-sensitive projects for Kenya's highland catchments
  • Reservoir Hydro & Multi-Purpose Dams - Large projects combining generation with irrigation and water supply
  • Small & Mini Hydro for Rural Electrification - Community-scale plants from 10 kW to 1 MW
  • Hydro Rehabilitation & Modernisation - Upgrading ageing turbines and control systems
  • Climate Resilience & Sediment Management - Designing for extreme hydrology and environmental compliance
