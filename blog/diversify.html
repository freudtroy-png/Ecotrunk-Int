Why Kenya Must Diversify Its Renewable Energy Portfolio

By Ecotrunk Strategy Team | 6 min read

Kenya is already a global leader in renewable energy, with over 90 percent of electricity generated from clean sources. However, the composition of this generation mix presents both opportunities and vulnerabilities that demand strategic attention.

The Current Mix

Geothermal provides approximately 45 percent of Kenya's electricity, hydropower around 35 percent, wind approximately 12 percent, and solar a growing but still modest share. While the high renewable share is commendable, the concentration risk is significant. Drought periods can reduce hydropower output by 40 percent or more, forcing reliance on expensive emergency diesel generation. Geothermal plants, while providing excellent baseload, require significant upfront capital and long development timelines.

The Case for Balance

A balanced renewable energy portfolio distributes risk across multiple technologies with complementary profiles. Solar generates during daylight hours when demand peaks. Wind often blows strongest at night and during cloudy periods, complementing solar. Hydropower with reservoir storage provides dispatchable capacity that can be called upon when needed. Geothermal provides stable baseload regardless of weather conditions. By developing all four technologies in a coordinated manner, Kenya can minimise the impact of any single technology's variability or disruption.

Our Recommendation

Ecotrunk advocates for a deliberate diversification strategy that sets minimum and maximum shares for each technology in the national generation mix. This approach would guide investment decisions, grid planning, and tariff policy towards an optimally balanced portfolio that maximises reliability, minimises cost, and reduces climate risk.
