Lessons from Lake Turkana: Building Africa's Largest Wind Farm

By Ecotrunk Engineering Team | 8 min read

The Lake Turkana Wind Power project stands as Africa's largest wind farm, with 365 turbines producing 310 MW of clean electricity. Located in one of Kenya's most remote and challenging environments, the project offers invaluable lessons for renewable energy development across the continent.

Engineering in Extreme Conditions

The Turkana Corridor experiences some of the strongest and most consistent winds on the continent, with peak speeds of 11 to 13 metres per second. However, the remote location demanded innovative logistics: all 365 turbines, each weighing hundreds of tonnes, were transported over 1,200 kilometres from Mombasa port through rough terrain. This required upgrading roads, building temporary bridges, and coordinating convoys across multiple counties. The lesson for future projects is clear: logistics planning must begin during feasibility, not after financial close.

Community Engagement as a Foundation

The project brought all-weather roads, reliable mobile phone coverage, and employment to one of Kenya's most marginalised regions. Community development agreements funded schools, clinics, and local infrastructure. Ecotrunk's approach mirrors this model: we view community engagement not as a regulatory box to tick but as a foundation for project success and long-term social license to operate.

Grid Integration Challenges

Integrating 310 MW of variable wind power into a grid designed around predictable hydropower and geothermal baseload required sophisticated forecasting, dispatch scheduling, and curtailment protocols. The project invested in a dedicated control centre and worked closely with Kenya Power and KETRACO to ensure grid stability. These integration lessons are directly applicable to Kenya's expanding renewable energy portfolio.
