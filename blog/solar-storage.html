Solar-Plus-Storage: The New Standard for Commercial Energy

By Ecotrunk Energy Storage Team | 10 min read

The convergence of falling solar module prices and dramatic battery cost reductions has created a tipping point for commercial energy in Kenya. Solar-plus-storage systems can now deliver firm, dispatchable power at a lower levelised cost than grid electricity or diesel generation for many commercial and industrial users. With Kenya's commercial electricity tariffs among the highest in East Africa and diesel prices subject to global volatility, solar-plus-storage has transitioned from an emerging technology to a financially compelling investment.

The Battery Cost Revolution

The single most important driver of the solar-plus-storage business case is the staggering decline in lithium-ion battery costs. According to BloombergNEF's annual battery price survey, lithium-ion battery pack prices have fallen by more than 80 percent over the past decade, from over USD 1,100 per kilowatt-hour in 2015 to approximately USD 139 per kilowatt-hour in 2024. This trajectory has consistently beaten analyst expectations and continues to decline as manufacturing scale expands, production processes improve, and material costs fall. At these price levels, adding storage to a solar installation increases the total system cost by roughly 30 to 50 percent but can double or even triple the useful energy output by capturing generation that would otherwise be curtailed during low-demand midday periods. For facilities with evening peak loads, unreliable grid supply, or expensive diesel backup, the combination of solar and storage now delivers a lower cost of energy than any single source.

LFP Chemistry: The New Industry Standard

Not all lithium-ion batteries are created equal. Lithium iron phosphate (LFP) chemistry has emerged as the dominant choice for stationary energy storage applications, and for good reason. Unlike nickel-manganese-cobalt (NMC) batteries commonly used in electric vehicles, LFP cells offer several critical advantages for commercial storage. First and foremost is safety: LFP's olivine crystal structure is thermally and chemically stable, making it extremely resistant to thermal runaway. This eliminates the fire risk that has plagued NMC installations and simplifies permitting and insurance requirements. Second is cycle life: LFP batteries routinely achieve 6,000 to 10,000 charge-discharge cycles at 80 percent depth of discharge, translating to 15 to 20 years of daily cycling in a commercial application. Third, LFP contains no cobalt, eliminating exposure to the ethical and supply chain issues associated with cobalt mining in the Democratic Republic of Congo. Fourth, LFP maintains consistent performance across a wide temperature range, a crucial advantage for installations in Kenya's varied climatic conditions, from the coastal heat of Mombasa to the highland chill of Nairobi.

System Design Considerations

Designing an optimal solar-plus-storage system requires careful evaluation of several technical parameters. The choice between DC coupling and AC coupling is one of the earliest design decisions. DC-coupled systems connect the solar array and battery on the same DC bus, using a single inverter for both. This configuration offers higher round-trip efficiency, typically 92 to 96 percent, because the solar energy avoids a double conversion. It is ideal for new installations where solar and storage are deployed simultaneously. AC-coupled systems connect the battery via a separate inverter, offering greater flexibility for retrofitting storage onto existing solar arrays. While round-trip efficiency is slightly lower at 88 to 92 percent due to the additional conversion step, AC coupling allows independent sizing and replacement of components. C-rate selection is another critical parameter. A 1C battery can discharge its full capacity in one hour, while a 0.5C battery takes two hours. For commercial peak shaving applications, a 0.5C to 1C rating is typically appropriate, allowing the battery to cover the evening load peak for two to four hours. Depth of discharge, or DoD, directly affects cycle life. Operating a battery between 10 and 90 percent state of charge rather than 0 to 100 percent can double the number of cycles before capacity degradation becomes significant. Most commercial systems are designed with a DoD of 80 to 90 percent, balancing usable capacity against longevity.

Peak Shaving in Practice: The Kenyan Commercial Case

The strongest near-term business case for solar-plus-storage in Kenya is peak shaving for commercial and industrial electricity consumers. Kenya Power's commercial tariff structure includes both energy charges and demand charges based on the highest 30-minute power draw in a billing period. A typical Nairobi office building might see its demand charge account for 30 to 50 percent of the total monthly electricity bill. Solar-plus-storage addresses both components. Solar generation offsets daytime energy consumption directly, while the battery charges from surplus solar during the day and discharges during the evening peak period, typically 6 pm to 9 pm, to flatten the facility's load profile and reduce demand charges. For manufacturing facilities operating shift patterns, the battery can also provide backup power during grid outages, replacing expensive and polluting diesel generators. A 100 kW solar array paired with a 200 kWh battery can reduce a facility's grid imports by 60 to 70 percent and eliminate the demand charge entirely. At current system prices of approximately USD 600 to 800 per kilowatt-hour installed, the payback period for a well-designed commercial system ranges from three to five years, with an internal rate of return of 15 to 25 percent over the system's 20-year design life. For facilities currently relying on diesel generators for backup, the fuel savings alone can deliver a payback under three years.

Grid Services and Ancillary Markets

Beyond behind-the-meter savings, solar-plus-storage systems can generate revenue by providing grid services. As Kenya's electricity grid integrates ever-higher shares of variable renewable generation, the need for fast-responding flexible resources grows. Battery storage is uniquely suited to provide frequency response services, injecting or absorbing power within milliseconds to maintain the grid's 50 Hz operating frequency. Voltage support is another valuable service, particularly in distribution networks where solar penetration is high and voltage fluctuations become a concern. Kenya's Energy Act 2019 and the subsequent EPRA regulations provide a framework for ancillary services markets, and while these markets are still in their early stages of development, early adopters with storage assets will be well-positioned to participate. The potential revenue from grid services, estimated at USD 10 to 30 per kilowatt per year in comparable markets, can improve project economics by 10 to 20 percent. Forward-thinking commercial clients installing storage today are effectively future-proofing their assets for participation in these emerging revenue streams.

Future Outlook: Beyond Lithium-Ion

While lithium-ion batteries dominate the current market, several emerging technologies promise to further expand the capabilities and reduce the costs of energy storage. Second-life electric vehicle batteries, retired from automotive service with 70 to 80 percent of their original capacity, offer a lower-cost option for stationary storage applications. With Kenya's EV market beginning to grow, a future supply of second-life batteries could provide affordable storage for rural mini-grids and smaller commercial installations. Flow batteries, including vanadium redox and iron-chromium chemistries, offer the advantages of unlimited cycle life, no capacity degradation over time, and scalable energy capacity independent of power capacity. For larger commercial and utility-scale applications requiring four to twelve hours of storage duration, flow batteries are becoming increasingly competitive. Looking further ahead, green hydrogen produced through electrolysis powered by surplus solar and wind energy offers a seasonal storage solution capable of shifting energy across weeks or months. While the round-trip efficiency of hydrogen storage remains low at 30 to 40 percent, it may prove essential for decarbonising industrial processes and providing long-duration storage as Kenya's renewable penetration approaches 100 percent. Solar-plus-storage is not merely a technology choice for today's commercial energy needs; it is a foundation for the clean, resilient, and affordable energy system of tomorrow.
